# Patterns and edge cases

These are fictional examples that illustrate structure, not claims of real findings. Substitute verified facts from the user's materials.

## Analytic exhibits

| Context-dependent | Reader-first | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The floor | Forecast price stabilizes near $3.99 | Names subject and level |
| The sign flips | X–Y correlation shifts from positive to negative across sample windows | States relationship and direction |
| The dense window | Vacancy rate drops to 6% in the 2023–24 sample | Uses sample as context, not subject |
| Holt's view | Holt model projects 1% annual growth | Method qualifies assertion |
| Opening band | Forecast interval widens from 4 to 10 points by year 5 | Makes uncertainty measurable |
| An inflection | Operating margin turns positive in Q3 | Identifies measurable event |
| Where we landed | Two scenarios meet the $5M budget ceiling | Communicates choice-relevant conclusion |
| The correlation story | The observed correlation is 0.76 in the latest sample | States observation |

## Document and section headings

| Generic / procedural | Reader-first |
|---|---|
| What we found | Three delays explain most of the missed launch milestones |
| An evolving landscape | Customer acquisition costs rose for three consecutive quarters |
| Methodology and insights | Sample selection excludes customers with fewer than 30 active days |
| Risk considerations | Vendor concentration creates a single point of failure |
| Future directions | Next quarter's priority is reducing manual reconciliation work |
| Why this matters | Higher forecast error increases the capital buffer required |

A document's top-level title can remain topical when it covers many claims, such as `2026 Operating Review: Demand, Margins, and Capital Allocation`. The executive summary then states the synthesized answer.

## Requests and correspondence

| Vague subject | Reader-first subject |
|---|---|
| Following up | Decision needed by Friday: contract renewal scope |

## Special cases

### No supported quantitative finding

Avoid forcing a number or direction into a topic. Use `Comparison of three onboarding models` when the evidence is still under review. Once evaluated, a supported takeaway could be `Model B cuts average onboarding time by 18%`.

### Mixed or conditional result

Do not claim `Discounting increases conversions` if only one segment shows an increase. Use `Discounting increases conversions for new customers, not repeat buyers` if validated by the evidence. If confidence is low, show it: `Early sample suggests higher conversion among new customers`.

### Correlation and causality

Prefer `X and Y moved together in 2025` to `X drove Y` without identification evidence. Do not describe a merely visual pattern as statistically significant.

### Methodology is the finding

Method titles are appropriate when the finding is about methods: `Holt model reduces forecast error by 12% relative to the baseline` is stronger than `Holt's view`.

### Technical audiences

Keep established domain terms where they improve precision. Do not replace `Net asset value reconciliation exceptions rose 20%` with `The books no longer balance`. Technical expertise does not supply missing context about a specific analysis.

### Interpretive, argumentative, and narrative work

An essay may use an intriguing question and develop the answer through the body. A literary headline can be evocative. The quality standard becomes *appropriate reader orientation for the genre*, not compulsory declarative statistics. Honor deliberate suspense when requested.

### Maps, processes, and instructional diagrams

When no inference is intended, use accurate task-oriented labels: `Customer onboarding: approval and provisioning steps`, not an invented business result.

### Dense dashboards

Use explicit metric-label-and-value pairings; not every tile needs a sentence. Units, timeframes, and comparison baselines must be visible nearby. A KPI tile named `At the floor` fails; `Occupancy | 82% | Q3` can pass if the reader knows the metric.

### Caveats that belong in the title

If a limitation changes the proposition, put it in the headline: `Revenue rose 8%, but only after the acquisition` is safer than `Revenue rose 8%` with the acquisition detail hidden in a footnote. A claim with a misleading omitted denominator fails the evidence-fidelity gate.
